What could have been one agent?

Ticket 102 could be handled simply by one well-prompted agent with get_lease, get_shop_date, and get_cash_account. It is a short chain: read the lease, compare the date, check cash, and prepare an approval request. Splitting Facilities and Accounting makes ownership clearer, but for one rent notice it adds a handoff without much specialist ambiguity.

Ticket 101 could also be one tool-using agent if the goal were only to report “size S is out, invoice 501 is open.” Ticket 103 is the strongest case for a team: stock, margin, and customer wording are meaningfully different jobs. Even there, a single agent could do the first analysis with product stock/price data and produce a draft, provided a separate human approval boundary remained in place.

Three problems this team could solve now

  1. Another out-of-stock customer order. Boss can route the request to Inventory, use get_product_stock_and_price, inspect an associated invoice/vendor, and ask Customer Service for a truthful draft. Ticket 101 is the working pattern.
  2. A new overdue vendor invoice. Accounting can use get_shop_date, get_invoice_with_vendor, and get_cash_account to prepare a payment recommendation. Like ticket 101, the payment would still wait for the human approval route.
  3. A second shop rent or lease notice. Facilities can use get_lease and get_shop_date, delegate the cash check to Accounting, and have Boss prioritize it. Ticket 102 proves the team has the relevant shape of data for this class of problem.

Three problems the current tools cannot solve

  1. Actually receiving or restocking the missing tee. The current MCP tools can identify zero stock and a vendor’s lead time, but cannot create a purchase order, update inventory, or confirm shipment. We would need an approved create_purchase_order mutation, vendor/order status tables, and an Inventory agent able to verify the resulting record.
  2. Sending the customer or landlord a real message. Customer Service can draft, but there is no email/SMS tool, delivery status, consent record, or human send-approval workflow. We would need a sandboxed communications MCP tool, message templates, recipient validation, and a human approval agent or route before sending.
  3. Processing a sale, refund, or card payment. The database models cash leaving the shop and has no revenue, orders, refunds, or payment processor records. The current Accounting agent cannot safely answer whether a customer paid or issue a refund. We would need order/revenue/refund tables, a payment-provider MCP integration, reconciliation tools, and stronger Accounting plus Customer Service workflows.

Bottom line

The team design is appropriately cautious and the Expected plans are focused rather than “send everyone everywhere.” But this run was an infrastructure failure: the MCP bridge must be fixed before agent quality can be judged. The next meaningful comparison is to rerun from the same $3,400 baseline, capture actual named MCP calls and delegations in the audit trail, and only then mark a ticket resolved or request human approval.
